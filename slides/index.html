First: / why clustering, / and how k-means works.

Good prototypes are close to their points

k-means minimizes one objective by two alternating updates

Goal Each prototype should stand for the points of its group.

We need one number for how well they do, and prototypes that make it small.

The objective $J$ measures the fit: the squared distance from each point to its prototype, summed over all points.

Minimize $J$ in turns: assign with the prototypes fixed, then average.

$$J=\underbrace{\class{t-s}{\sum_i\sum_c}}_{\substack{\text{all points}\\\text{and clusters}}}\;\underbrace{\class{t-u}{u_{ci}}}_{\substack{1\text{ if }i\text{ is in }c\text{,}\\\text{else }0}}\;\underbrace{\class{t-d}{\|\bm{x}_i-\bm{b}_c\|^2}}_{\substack{\text{squared}\\\text{distance}}}$$
$$\underbrace{\class{t-ua}{u_{ci}=1\ \text{for}\ c=\mathop{\arg\min}_k\|\bm{x}_i-\bm{b}_k\|^2}}_{\text{fix }\bm{b}\text{: the nearest prototype}}$$ $$\underbrace{\class{t-bm}{\bm{b}_c=\frac{\sum_i u_{ci}\bm{x}_i}{\sum_i u_{ci}}}}_{\text{fix }u\text{: the mean}}$$

k-means is a clustering method that represents each cluster by the mean of its points.

$\bm{x}_i$: data point $i$; $\bm{b}_c$: the prototype of cluster $c$.$C$: the number of clusters (here $C=3$).

$J$ never increases.→ But k-means splits the clusters with a crisp boundary.

k-means splits clusters with a crisp boundary

FCM expresses the boundary by degrees

Problem k-means forces a point halfway between two clusters to pick one.

So we let membership be a degree in $[0,1]$.

The same $J$ as k-means, but membership is a degree, softened by the power $m$.

Both updates minimize $J$: degrees from distances, prototypes as weighted means.

$$J=\sum_i\sum_c \underbrace{\class{t-u}{u_{ci}^{\,m}}}_{\text{degree, to the power }m}\|\bm{x}_i-\bm{b}_c\|^2$$
$$\underbrace{u_{ci}=\frac{\|\bm{x}_i-\bm{b}_c\|^{-2/(m-1)}}{\sum_k\|\bm{x}_i-\bm{b}_k\|^{-2/(m-1)}}}_{\text{nearer, larger; summing to 1}}$$ $$\underbrace{\bm{b}_c=\frac{\sum_i u_{ci}^{\,m}\,\bm{x}_i}{\sum_i u_{ci}^{\,m}}}_{\text{weighted mean}}$$

Idea The nearer a prototype, the larger the degree; each point’s degrees add up to 1.

FCM (fuzzy c-means) is a clustering method that gives each point a degree of membership in every cluster.

$u_{ci}\in[0,1]$, summing to 1 over the clusters.$m>1$ is the fuzzifier: the larger $m$, the softer the degrees.

$J$ never increases.→ Part 1: in rough c-means (RCM), a point between clusters belongs to both.

In rough set theory, neighborhoods give rise to uncertainty

Rough sets judge each point by its neighborhood

Insight A point is seen only through its neighbors: if they are in different clusters, its cluster is uncertain.

So we judge each point by its neighborhood, not by distance.

The neighborhood of a point: every point within a radius $\delta$, itself included.

$$\underbrace{\mathcal{N}_i}_{\text{the neighborhood of }i}=\{\,j:\ \|\bm{x}_i-\bm{x}_j\|\le \delta\,\}$$

Idea If all neighbors of a point are in a cluster, the point is certainly in it; if only some are, possibly.

As $\delta$ shrinks, $\mathcal{N}_i$ becomes pure and $i$ certain.→ Rough membership turns the neighborhood into a degree.

Rough membership grades a point by its neighbors

Rough membership is the share of a point's neighbors that are in the cluster.

All neighbors in the cluster: certain; neighbors in both: possibly in either.

$$\mu_{ci}=\underbrace{\frac{|\mathcal{N}_i\cap X_c|}{|\mathcal{N}_i|}}_{\text{share of cluster }c\text{ among the neighbors}}$$
$$\underbrace{\mu_{ci}=1}_{\text{certainly in }c}$$
$$\underbrace{0<\mu_{ci}<1}_{\text{possibly in }c\text{ (boundary)}}$$

RMCM2 puts rough membership into one objective

Ours

RMCM2 is k-means with rough membership in place of the crisp assignment.

Minimize $J$ in turns: labels by the smaller cost $q$, prototypes as $\mu$-weighted means.

$$J=\sum_c\sum_i \underbrace{\class{t-mu}{\mu_{ci}}}_{\substack{\text{rough membership,}\\\text{in place of }u}}\|\bm{x}_i-\bm{b}_c\|^2$$
$$q_{cj}=\underbrace{\sum_i P_{ij}\|\bm{x}_i-\bm{b}_c\|^2}_{\substack{\bm{b}_c\text{'s error over the points}\\\text{with }j\text{ in their neighborhoods}}}$$ $$\underbrace{\bm{b}_c=\frac{\sum_i \mu_{ci}\,\bm{x}_i}{\sum_i \mu_{ci}}}_{\mu\text{-weighted mean}}$$

$P_{ij}$: the weight of $j$ in $\mathcal{N}_i$ ($1/|\mathcal{N}_i|$, or 0 outside).→ An application: recommendation.

Ubukata, Notsu, Honda, Inf. Sci., 2021

RMCM2 / puts rough membership into one objective.
Keep the k-means objective, / but weigh each error by rough membership.
Then derive every update from it.
This is RMCM2.
The lines are the errors in J, / each weighted by mu.
A point between the clusters / sends its error to both.
RMCM2 / is a clustering method / that minimizes the k-means objective, / with mu in place of u.

(→) Minimizing J over the labels: / each point takes the cluster with the smaller cost q.
q_cj adds up the errors, to prototype c, / of the points with j in their neighborhoods: / the dashed circle.
So point j goes to the cluster / that best fits the points with j as a neighbor.
P_ij is the weight of j / in the neighborhood of i: / one over its size, / or 0 outside it.
Minimizing J over the prototypes: / each one is a mu-weighted mean.
Here, / RMCM2 runs from the start, / and J never increases.

The SOM orders prototypes on a lattice

Each input pulls its winner, and the winner's lattice neighbors with it.

$$\underbrace{\bm{b}_c}_{\text{node }c}\leftarrow\bm{b}_c+\underbrace{\alpha}_{\text{rate}}\,\underbrace{h(c,w)}_{\substack{w\text{: the winner,}\\\text{nearest to }\bm{x}}}\,\underbrace{(\bm{x}-\bm{b}_c)}_{\substack{\text{toward}\\\text{the input}}}$$ $$\underbrace{h(c,w)=e^{-\|\bm{r}_c-\bm{r}_w\|^2/2\sigma^2}}_{\substack{\text{pulls the winner's neighbors too}\\\bm{r}_c\text{: node }c\text{ on the lattice; }\sigma\text{: reach}}}$$

Each node moves to a weighted mean of all points.

$$\bm{b}_c=\frac{\sum_i h(c,w_i)\,\bm{x}_i}{\sum_i h(c,w_i)}$$ $$\underbrace{w_i=\mathop{\arg\min}_k\|\bm{x}_i-\bm{b}_k\|^2}_{\text{the winner of point }i}$$

The shading on the right is $h(c,w)$: largest at the winner, less for its lattice neighbors.

On the lattice, a point can only sit on its winner node.→ Can each point have its own position on the lattice?

SOM-OLP adds one variable: the latent position

SOM-OLP minimizes one objective in closed form

Given The SOM's prototypes $\bm{b}$ and memberships $u$ (soft winners), plus a new variable: each point's latent position $\bm{v}_i$.

Find all three by minimizing one objective, so that no update makes it worse.

Ours

One objective: data fit, lattice distance of each position $\bm{v}$, entropy of $u$.

Fix two blocks of variables and minimize over the third, in closed form.

$$J=\sum_{i,c}u_{ci}\bigl(\underbrace{\class{t-fit}{\|\bm{x}_i-\bm{b}_c\|^2}}_{\text{data fit}}+\underbrace{\class{t-lat}{\gamma\|\bm{v}_i-\bm{r}_c\|^2}}_{\text{lattice distance}}+\underbrace{\class{t-ent}{\lambda\ln u_{ci}}}_{\text{entropy}}\bigr)$$
$$\underbrace{\class{t-bu}{u_{ci}\propto \exp(-q_{ci}/\lambda)}}_{\text{softmax}}$$ $$\underbrace{\class{t-bv}{\bm{v}_i=\sum_c u_{ci}\,\bm{r}_c}}_{\text{mean of nodes}}$$ $$\underbrace{\class{t-bb}{\bm{b}_c=\frac{\sum_i u_{ci}\bm{x}_i}{\sum_i u_{ci}}}}_{\text{mean of data}}$$

Supplement: Heskes's SOM minimizes one energy

Problem SOM updates are rules that decrease no known function.

So we want SOM learning to minimize one energy, as k-means does.

One energy for all the errors, each winner chosen to make its own term smallest.

$$E=\sum_i\sum_c \underbrace{h(c,w_i)}_{\text{near the winner}}\|\bm{x}_i-\bm{b}_c\|^2$$ $$\underbrace{w_i=\mathop{\arg\min}_c\sum_k h(c,k)\,\|\bm{x}_i-\bm{b}_k\|^2}_{\text{the winner minimizes its term of }E}$$

Idea Choose each winner by how well its whole lattice neighborhood fits the point: then one energy is minimized.

Batch SOM on the surface: its updates are rules, with no objective.

On the same surface and lattice, the energy-based winner can differ from the nearest node: for 363 of 800 points (with $h$ of width $\sigma$ = 1).
